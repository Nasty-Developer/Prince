The first forty-eight hours after a rescue

The most dangerous part of a rescue is not the road. It is the night after, when a frightened dog is warm, fed and quietly getting worse.

6 min read ·Educational field note

In short

  • Warmth, water and quiet come before food and before introductions.
  • Refeeding a starving dog too fast can kill it.
  • A vet check within 24 hours, even if the dog looks fine.

Shock first, everything else second

A dog lifted off the street is often in shock: cold, dehydrated, and running on adrenaline. The first hours are not about cuddles. They are about a quiet space, a steady temperature and no handling beyond what is necessary.

Keep the dog away from other animals. Not because it is dirty, but because it is stressed and unvaccinated.

Warm, then water, then food

A cold dog cannot digest. Warm the space first: a blanket, a covered box, a room without draughts. Then offer small amounts of water. A dog that has been without water for a day can vomit if it drinks a bowl at once.

Food comes in small, frequent portions. Rice with a little boiled chicken or curd is fine for a first meal. Rich food after starvation causes diarrhoea, and diarrhoea in a weak dog is dangerous.

Safety: Do not free-feed a severely thin dog. Two tablespoons every few hours is safer than a full bowl, and the vet should guide the first days.

Observe, write it down

In the first day, note what you actually see: appetite, water intake, stool, urine, breathing rate, whether the dog can stand, whether it reacts to sound, and every wound. Photograph the wounds once, in good light, and again tomorrow.

These notes are what the vet needs. "He seems okay" is not information. "Ate 40g at 6pm, drank, passed soft stool at 9pm, breathing 34 per minute at rest" is.

The vet visit

Within 24 hours, and the same day if there is any wound, discharge, lameness, coughing, or if the dog is a puppy. The vet will check for ticks, mange, worms, ear and skin infection, and will advise on vaccination timing given the dog's condition.

A dog can look fine on day one and crash on day three. The check is not a formality.

The quiet week

The first week is not the time for visitors, photographs or introductions to your own dog. Let the dog sleep, eat and learn that nothing in this house hurts.

Most early "behaviour problems" are just a dog that has not slept properly in weeks.

Rescue does not end when the dog is in the car. It starts there, and the first night is the one that matters most.
