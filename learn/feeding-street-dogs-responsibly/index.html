Feeding & Nutrition

Feeding street dogs responsibly

Feeding changes a street. Done well it improves health and trust; done badly it creates conflict, dependence and disease. The difference is routine.

6 min read · Reviewed guidance for India

Key points

  • Fixed spot, fixed time, plates removed.
  • Water daily, in shade, in a heavy shallow bowl.
  • Feeding is the doorway to vaccination and sterilization, not the end of the work.

Feed a dog, not a crowd

Scattering food over a wide area pulls dogs from several streets into one place, and that is where fights, complaints and traffic accidents start. Feed in a fixed spot, away from the road, and remove the plates afterwards.

One bowl per dog, spaced apart, reduces guarding and lets the shy dog eat.

What to give

Street dogs do well on ordinary food: rice or roti with curd, boiled egg, a little boiled chicken, or a decent commercial dog food if the budget allows. Clean water matters more than people expect, especially in summer.

Avoid what makes them ill: sugary biscuits and cream biscuits as a staple, spicy gravy, fried food, milk in quantity, chocolate, onion, garlic, grapes and raisins, and cooked bones that splinter.

How much and how often

Once or twice a day at fixed times is better than constant grazing, because it lets you notice when a dog stops eating, which is often the first sign of illness.

Do not feed so much that the dog stops scavenging entirely without a plan for the days you cannot come. A feeder who disappears is a real risk, so keep the routine simple enough that someone else can cover it.

Water is the neglected half

In Indian summers, a shallow heavy bowl of clean water in shade saves more lives than food. Change it daily, scrub it, and keep it out of the sun.

Do not leave water in deep buckets where a puppy can fall in.

Feeding and vaccination belong together

A dog you can feed is a dog you can eventually vaccinate and sterilize. Feeding without that follow-up grows the population without protecting it.

When the municipal ABC van or a vaccination camp comes to your area, being the person the dogs trust is what makes the catch possible.

When to call a veterinarian

  • A dog that stops eating for more than 24 hours.
  • Repeated vomiting or diarrhoea, especially in a puppy.
  • Visible weight loss despite regular feeding.
