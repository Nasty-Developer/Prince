Adoption Guide

Adopting a street dog

Adoption fails for predictable reasons. These are the questions worth answering honestly before a dog moves in, and the first month that makes it stick.

7 min read · Reviewed guidance for India

Key points

  • Everyone in the house must genuinely agree.
  • Budget for vet costs, not just food.
  • Three days, three weeks, three months.
  • Agree in writing that the dog returns to the rescue if it does not work.

The honest questions

Who is home during the day, and for how many hours? What happens to the dog when you travel, when you are ill, or if you move? Does everyone in the household actually want a dog, including the person who will do the 6am walk?

If the answer to any of these is "we will figure it out", the honest position is not yet.

Cost and time, in real terms

A dog costs food, vaccination, sterilization, deworming, and a vet fund for the year, plus a collar, lead, and a place to sleep. Time is the larger cost: two walks a day, training, and the first weeks of supervision.

A rescued street dog is not a cheaper dog. It is a dog with a history, and history takes a little more patience at the start.

Meeting the dog

Meet away from the shelter noise if possible, and walk together rather than sitting face to face. Watch how the dog recovers from a startle, whether it takes food from your hand, and whether it seeks contact or avoids it.

One visit is not enough to know. Two or three, on different days, tells you far more than a single emotional hour.

The first month

Expect three days of shutdown, three weeks of settling, three months before the dog's real personality shows. During that time keep the routine boring: same food, same walk, same sleep spot, few visitors.

Do not take the new dog to a park full of dogs in week one. Do not leave it alone for eight hours in week one. Build the routine first, then widen the world.

What good follow-up looks like

A rescue that hands over a dog and disappears is not finished. Ask for a check-in call at one week, one month and three months, and say plainly that the dog comes back to the rescue rather than being abandoned if it does not work.

A return is a failure of matching, not a crime. It is far better than a dog on the road again.

When to call a veterinarian

  • A newly adopted dog that is not eating by day three, or is vomiting or passing bloody stool.
  • Any sign of illness in the first two weeks, since stress lowers immunity.
