Vaccination & Deworming

Vaccination and deworming, explained simply

Rabies is the one that matters most for a street dog and for the street. Here is what the vaccines are, when they are given, and what to say at a camp.

6 min read · Reviewed guidance for India

Key points

  • Rabies vaccination is the priority for any street dog.
  • Puppies get a course, not a single shot.
  • Deworming is cheap, safe and visibly effective.
  • Keep a written record per dog.

Rabies first

For a street dog in India, the rabies vaccine is the priority. It protects the dog and it protects everyone who lives near it. It is given from about three months of age, with a booster, and then annually.

A dog that has been vaccinated can be marked: many rescuers record the date, or the locality keeps a simple list. It prevents the same dog being caught and vaccinated repeatedly while a neighbour goes unvaccinated.

The core vaccines

Beyond rabies, the standard combination protects against distemper, parvovirus, adenovirus and leptospirosis, with parainfluenza. Puppies get a course of three shots a few weeks apart, then a booster at a year, then periodically.

A street dog who is already an adult and unvaccinated can be started at any age. Being unwell delays vaccination, not age.

Worms are the invisible problem

Most street puppies carry roundworms. Deworming is a simple tablet, repeated on a schedule, and it changes a puppy's body condition within weeks.

Pregnant bitches and puppies need a vet-guided schedule rather than a guess, because the dose depends on weight.

What to expect at a camp

Municipal and NGO camps run vaccine and sterilization drives regularly. Bring the dog calm and contained, and ask what is being given and when the next dose is due.

A dog that is sick, feverish or severely thin is usually deferred until it is stronger. That is correct practice, not a refusal.

Records do more than memory

Keep a simple note per dog: locality, description, vaccine, date, and who did it. Ten dogs and a year later, memory fails.

This is also what makes a locality's care visible to a municipal body when the ABC programme is being planned.

When to call a veterinarian

  • A dog with a fever, discharge or severe thinness should be vaccinated once stable.
  • A puppy with vomiting or diarrhoea needs a vet before any vaccine.
  • Any bite to a person needs immediate medical care, whatever the dog's history.
