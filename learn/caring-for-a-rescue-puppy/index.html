Puppy Care

Caring for a rescue puppy

Puppies die from small mistakes. Warmth, feeding, worms, vaccination and sleep are the five things that matter in the first weeks.

7 min read · Reviewed guidance for India

Key points

  • Warm first, then water, then small frequent meals.
  • Never cow's milk.
  • Assume worms; a vet sets the schedule.
  • Sleep is part of treatment.

Warmth before food, always

A puppy cannot regulate its own temperature well. A cold puppy will not digest food and can fade within hours. Use a covered box, blankets, and a warm water bottle wrapped in cloth, changed regularly.

Never put a puppy directly against a heater or hot water bottle without a cloth barrier.

Feeding by age

Under four weeks, a puppy needs a commercial puppy milk replacer, never cow's milk, fed by bottle at the intervals on the pack. From four weeks, soften puppy food with warm water into a mash and offer it several times a day.

From eight weeks, four small meals a day of puppy food. A puppy's stomach is small; one large meal causes vomiting and bloat.

Worms, ticks and the first vet visit

Assume worms. A vet will weigh the puppy and give an age-appropriate dewormer, then a schedule. Ticks are removed properly, not pulled and squeezed with fingers.

Vaccination starts around six to eight weeks depending on the vet's assessment. A puppy with diarrhoea or fever waits until stable.

Sleep and socialisation

Puppies sleep up to twenty hours a day. A puppy that is constantly handled by visitors is a puppy that never rests, and rest is when it grows.

Socialisation means calm exposure, not a party. Between three and sixteen weeks, introduce different floor surfaces, sounds, and gentle handling for a few minutes at a time.

The three emergencies

Diarrhoea in a puppy, especially with vomiting, dehydrates fast and can kill within a day. Refusing food for more than twelve hours is a warning. A puppy that is suddenly limp, cold or unresponsive needs a vet now, not in the morning.

Keep the vet number where you can find it at 2am.

When to call a veterinarian

  • Diarrhoea or vomiting, particularly with lethargy.
  • Refusing food for 12 hours or more.
  • Any puppy that is cold, limp or unresponsive.
  • Suspected parvovirus: bloody diarrhoea, vomiting, sudden collapse.
